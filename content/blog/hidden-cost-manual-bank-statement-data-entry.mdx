# The Hidden Cost of Manual Bank Statement Data Entry

Every hour spent manually entering bank transactions is an hour not spent on your actual business. But the real cost of manual data entry isn't just time—it's the errors that creep in and compound.

## The Math on Manual Entry

A typical small business checking account generates 100–300 transactions per month. At 30–60 seconds per transaction (open statement, find the next entry, type the date, description, and amount, assign a category), that's roughly 2–5 hours of pure data entry per month. If you have multiple accounts—checking, savings, a business credit card—double or triple that.

That's 25–60 hours per year spent typing numbers from one format into another. At even a modest hourly rate, that's thousands of dollars in labor cost for a task that adds no strategic value.

## The Error Problem

Studies on manual data entry consistently show error rates between 1% and 5%. On 200 transactions per month, that's 2 to 10 errors. Most are small—a transposed digit, a decimal in the wrong place, a date off by one day. But small errors compound.

A $540 charge entered as $450 means your books are off by $90. If that error isn't caught during reconciliation, it cascades: your expense reports are wrong, your tax deductions are wrong, and if you're ever audited, you're explaining discrepancies instead of moving on.

The worst errors are the invisible ones: a transaction entered twice, or a transaction missed entirely. Duplicates inflate your expenses. Missing transactions hide income. Both create problems that surface months later when they're much harder to trace back to the source.
