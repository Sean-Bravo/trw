## The Hidden Cost of "We'll Just Parse It Ourselves"

Every fintech product that touches user financial data hits the same wall: file imports.

Your users want to upload a CSV from Coinbase, a transaction export from Kraken, or a bank statement PDF from Chase. Your product needs to turn that file into structured data. How hard can it be?

Harder than anyone expects.

A typical crypto exchange CSV has non-standard headers, inconsistent date formats, duplicate rows from API pagination bugs, and columns that change meaning between export types. Bank statement PDFs are worse — scanned images, multi-line transaction descriptions, and page headers repeated on every page that break naive table extraction.

Most teams underestimate this by 10x. What starts as "a weekend project" becomes a full-time maintenance burden.

## The Parser Maintenance Trap

Here's how it usually goes. Your team writes a Coinbase parser. It works. Then a user uploads a Binance CSV and it breaks because Binance uses different column names and a different date format. So you write a Binance parser. Then Kraken. Then KuCoin. Then Gemini.

Each exchange is a special case. And they don't hold still — Coinbase changed their export format twice in 2025 alone. Every format change breaks your parser, generates support tickets, and pulls an engineer off feature work to fix it.

For bank statements, multiply that problem by 50+ banks, add OCR complexity for scanned documents, and factor in the joy of multi-page tables with wrapped text.

Teams building this in-house typically report spending 200-400 engineering hours on initial parser development across exchanges and banks, plus 5-10 hours per month on maintenance when formats change.
