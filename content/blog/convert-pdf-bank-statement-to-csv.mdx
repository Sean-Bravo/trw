# How to Convert PDF Bank Statements to Clean CSV (Without Losing Data)

Your bank gave you a PDF. Your accounting software wants a CSV. And somewhere in the middle, transactions are going to get mangled, dates will flip around, and amounts will lose their decimal places. This is one of the most common data-wrangling problems in personal finance and small business bookkeeping.

## Why PDF-to-CSV Is Harder Than It Looks

A PDF bank statement is essentially a picture of a table. Unlike a CSV or Excel file, there's no underlying data structure—just text positioned at specific coordinates on a page. When a converter tries to extract the data, it has to figure out which text belongs to which column, where one row ends and another begins, and how to handle multi-line transaction descriptions.

This is where cheap or generic PDF converters fail. They treat every PDF the same, whether it's a bank statement, an invoice, or a research paper. Bank statements have specific challenges: multi-page tables that span page breaks, running balances that aren't transaction amounts, header/footer text that repeats on every page, and transaction descriptions that wrap to two or three lines.

## What to Look For in a Converter

The best bank statement converters are purpose-built for financial documents. They understand that a bank statement has a specific structure: date, description, debits, credits, and balance columns. They know to skip page headers and footers, handle multi-line descriptions, and preserve the correct number of decimal places in amounts.

Key features that matter: automatic bank detection (so it knows how to parse a Chase statement differently from a Bank of America statement), OCR capability for scanned documents, multi-page handling without losing transactions at page breaks, and output formatting for specific targets like QuickBooks or Xero.

Features that don't matter as much: fancy dashboards, built-in analytics, and AI-powered categorization. If the extraction is wrong, no amount of analysis on top will help. Accuracy comes first.
